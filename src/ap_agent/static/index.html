Invoice / AP Document Intelligence Agent

Trida AI Blueprint · Built on NVIDIA NIM · synthetic data · drafts stop at a finance approver — nothing is approved or paid by the agent

Pick a sample invoice and analyse it. The full pipeline runs: OCR → vision verify → three-way match → triage → diagnosis → drafts → guardrails → approver gate.

AP aging queue (awaiting approver)

Invoices waiting at the gate, most overdue first, with the triage mix.

Escalation digest (today's escalations, composed — never sent)

The day's escalations in one summary: recovery cases past their SLA, escalated postings, stuck invoices, four-eyes stalls, forecast lapses and realised drift — plus the standing governance lines: four-eyes wait budgets breached this month, onboarding changes whose decision windows are closing, batch groups the bank has not answered (bank silence), and the lag drift watch's announcements — a statistic degrading, or standing excluded, named with its recalls. Composed from the records; the agent sends nothing.
Storing pins the day's first-computed digest verbatim — the copy a human forwards or files. First-computed wins: a stored day is never overwritten, and a live recomputation is always labelled "computed". "Store due digests" backfills the bounded lookback (7 days) for mornings nobody stored, attributed to "system".

Payment runs (proposals — the agent never pays)

Groups the approved invoices by currency + due window. Confirming records the lead's acceptance; the AP system of record executes the run. A run above the four-eyes threshold also needs a second, different confirmer before its hand-off file can be generated.

Hand-off profiles (governed routing — per supplier / per currency)

The payee side's say in the export: which format its bank or ERP ingests, how many lead days a pain.001 requested execution date needs, whether its bank rejects at-import creditor markers, and whether it books per value date (batch booking: one PmtInf per requested execution date, the lead days flooring every group's date). /metrics grades each profile — exports routed, marker rate against ungoverned exports, flag follow-through, lead promises kept. Setting a profile names its actor and appends to the profile's audit trail. Exports resolve it deterministically — a format named on the export wins, then the currency profile, then a supplier profile the run's members agree on, else the CSV default. A supplier whose bank rejects markers is flagged on the run at proposal time while the master holds no bank details for it.

A grade that names configuration theatre — a profile governing no exports, or routing no safer than ungoverned with its marker flag off — becomes a governed proposal here: drafted from the evidence by one operator, decided by a second (drafter ≠ decider), applied only on approval. A retirement deletes the profile; a tightening sets markers_rejected through the profile's own path.
The drift forecast's lag statistics are graded against the export ledger, and the drift watch demotes a statistic whose recent recall decays — automatically, run by run. A standing ruling is a governed proposal here: a degrading statistic drafts its retirement from the forecast's candidate set; an excluded segment whose recent record recovered drafts its re-grade back in. Drafter ≠ decider; the exclusion state is derived from the decided proposals themselves.
The re-analysis analytics name the suppliers whose replies never move their cases — the settling document was never collected. A governed proposal here answers the pattern: a supplier repeatedly not unblocked drafts a collect-documents-up-front change to their onboarding checklist; a listed supplier whose re-analyses since listing all moved their cases drafts its release. The listings are graded in /metrics — information waits and clarification loops per case, before vs after the listing moment — and a listing that moved nothing drafts its own release on the grade's evidence; approving that release re-grades first, so a listing that started working is never released on a stale grade. Drafter ≠ decider; who stands listed is derived from the decided proposals themselves, and a listed supplier's information waits say so in the queue.

Approved, not yet posted (ERP loop open items)

Approved invoices with no posted receipt past the grace window — the postings the ERP still owes an answer for. A failed receipt keeps its item open, with the ERP's reason. Escalating climbs the ladder: the signed posting_escalation webhook past the escalation threshold, then posting_escalation_urgent past twice it if the posting is still open — once per rung per open episode, recorded on the invoice record. /metrics grades the ladder: which rung a posting followed (a posted receipt after rung 1 credits rung 1; after rung 2, rung 2 — the first shout had its window), which episodes closed without it, and which stand open exactly as they stood when the last rung fired.

Supplier master changes (governed — proposer ≠ approver)

A bank-detail change is proposed here, decided by a second operator with the out-of-band verification reference recorded, and applied only from its effective date. Until then the fraud screen keeps matching documents against the current record — the new details still read as a mismatch. A request nobody decides within the expiry window (30 days by default) expires and must be re-proposed.

Proposing from an invoice turns the bank details its documents state into a pending request — nothing is applied by proposing.

Supplier onboarding worklist (marker risk, ranked)

Suppliers ranked by the export value that still carried the SUPPLIER-MASTER-AT-IMPORT marker — the fraud screen's blind spot as a work queue. Onboarding a supplier is the governed change above; the button prefills it. Suppliers the master now covers drop off and are counted as resolved.
